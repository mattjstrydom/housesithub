A different way to travel

Skip the hotel strip. Stay in an actual neighbourhood, slow down, and spend a lot less doing it.

House sitting means staying in a real home instead of a hotel room: shopping at the local market, getting to know the street, and settling into the everyday rhythm of a place instead of rushing through it. And because you're looking after a home and its pets while the owners are away, it costs a lot less too.

There's a bit of a learning curve to doing it well. We've mapped it out, so you don't have to learn it the hard way like we did.

The course

Learn How to House Sit

Learn How to House Sit is a course for people who want to slow down, spend real time in one place, and experience a country more like a local than a tourist passing through, all while spending a lot less than a hotel would cost.

It comes from 22 house sits our family of four has done across six countries and counting, plus 23 years working in training and course design. It covers how to find sits that actually suit you, how to write an application that gets noticed, what to check before you say yes, how to handle the pet and home care day to day, and how to leave things in a way that gets you invited back.

By the end, you'll know exactly what to do for your first sit, and you'll have the reviews to show for it.

Questions

A Few Common Questions

Is this course just about TrustedHousesitters? +

That's the platform we've used and had the most success with, so it comes up a lot in the course. But what you learn works just as well on other platforms or with a private arrangement.

Do I need to own a home or have pets myself to be a good sitter? +

No. You don't need any of that. The course walks you through what to expect, whatever your background.

Is house sitting actually free? +

Most sits are unpaid. You look after the home and pets, and in exchange, you get a place to stay. Occasionally, a longer sit might ask for a small contribution toward utilities. We cover this honestly in the course.
